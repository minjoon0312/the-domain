Travelling between world-lines with technology

Thought moves you a little. Machines move you a lot. Fixed portals, the alien implant that carried MM for decades, the dash-cam glimpses of other people's crossings, John Titor's "time machine", and MM's do-it-yourself portal theory, all explained as one operation: change the coordinates, not the place.

AI summary · part 12 of 40 6 min read · 1,303 words 29 sources ↓

Written by an AI, not by Metallicman. This is a plain-language summary of 29 archived articles (mainly the collections World-Line Travel Index, DIY – Do It Yourself Dimensional Portal Construction and Techniques, John Titor Index). It restates what those sources say and does not add claims of its own. Every part links to its sources at the bottom so you can check the original wording.

In one breath

In this model nothing ever travels through space. A world-line is a frozen snapshot that includes a place, a moment and a version of events, so "going to Paris in 1850" and "going to a world where the Beatles never broke up" are the same kind of move: you change the coordinates your consciousness is tuned to, and you re-entangle with the new snapshot. Thought does this in tiny steps. Technology does it in big ones, by forcing your consciousness from particle form into wave form and back at a chosen set of coordinates.

Five levels of travel

Three ways to force the wave

MM's summary of every technique he knows: "We can accomplish this in different ways."

1 · piggy-backConnect to a species that can do it

Some soul types cross world-lines natively. Entangle a human with one of them and the human goes where the host goes. This is what the EBP did to MM: after 1981 the benefactors "move me about as they desire".

2 · fixed portalPortal to portal

A large fixed installation. You walk into a field and vanish; you arrive at another portal or coordinate. MAJestic had one at the Pensacola ELF facility in 1981; observers can watch people enter and disappear.

3 · mobile bubbleA vehicle-sized field

"A mobile mechanism, resulting in a bubble where the bubble changes the reality surrounding it." John Titor's machine, in MM's reading, and the dash-cam crossings at road intersections.

ΨORM's one-line version: "A portal does not move you through space, it moves you through frozen moments." And a hard limit from the Commander (part 7 of the comm): an inmate suit "is not sustainable outside of the Prison Complex". The Domain has teleportation facilities on Earth and has never moved an inmate outside the walls in their suit.

What it felt like

  • Nothing, then details. MM never felt the switch. "The transition point is ALWAYS hidden from the observer." He would notice afterwards: breakfast places serving eggs with potatoes, trousers with buttons instead of zippers, a billboard for a strange surgery, the ending of a public story.
  • Dives and slides. A slide was a minor change of reality. A "dive" was a series of slides "that went all over the place", ending somewhere else. Both lasted hours to weeks and came without warning, once in the middle of a presentation to his managers.
  • Manual mode. When he ran the core kit #2 probes himself, a heads-up display showed seven soft numbers that "hardened" as he slid; deviation was locked to about two percent. He could not, for example, choose a "cat-friendly world-line" on purpose.
  • No twins. He never met a duplicate of himself. "Once you left your world-line and entered a new world-line", the old body is not left behind; the reality changes around one consciousness.
  • A price for distance. "If I were to go to a world line of say 30% deviance, and return, my consciousness will be affected." That, and his standing orders, are why he never explored.

Other people's crossings

MM collects public examples and reads them through the model: a woman in a foil-like coat walking out of nothing onto a Russian road (a dash-cam clip; her clothing suggests crude equipment and radiation shielding), vehicles appearing and vanishing at intersections ("roads and intersections provide stable coordinates"), a man who claimed to have been rescued by a stranger with a portal machine and came home with a Beatles tape from a world where the band never split, and "ProfessorPhate", who in 1999 described a different world-line online. His standard: the "unifying commonality" of such accounts is more useful than any single one, and travellers "look plain on purpose".

John Titor

In 2000 and 2001 a poster calling himself John Titor claimed to be a soldier from 2036 sent back for an IBM 5100 computer, and described a "time displacement unit" in a car. MM devotes four articles plus the full transcripts to him, and takes his side: "I happen to believe his story. I do believe that he is leaving certain things out." His reasons are the physical effort behind the hoax (photographs, a cross-section, manual pages, a laser-bending photo hard to fake in 1998) and the fit with his own experience: Titor's "time travel" is world-line travel with the time coordinate changed, and Titor himself said his world diverged from ours. MM uses the story as the reader's on-ramp: "Without it, the reader would have to make a jump directly into my training."

The do-it-yourself portal

Across ten articles MM lays out how he thinks a home-built dimensional portal would work. It is theory with engineering flavour, not a tested build, and he says so ("I recommend that a rent-an-engineer be utilized to design up the system"). The logic follows directly from the model.

How to read the DIY series

The articles quote real techniques (wavelet separation of gravity anomalies, hydromagnetic waves, ponderomotive force, Alan Holt's NASA field-resonance propulsion proposal) and connect them by MM's model. None of the steps has been demonstrated as a portal by anyone in the archive, and the concerns MM himself lists (secondary resonance, magnetohydrodynamic shock) are waved past as "minor". Treat it as a thought experiment written by an engineer, which is what he calls it.

Remember

  • All travel is coordinate change: place, time and version of events are one address.
  • Thought = level 0; fixed portals, mobile bubbles and entanglement with a capable species do the rest.
  • MM's own travel was passive (carried by the EBP), boring, unannounced, and capped at about 2% in manual mode.
  • Titor is used as an on-ramp; the DIY portal is an untested design study.
