Mysteries and OOPARTs: why the official history is doubted

A hammer inside rock older than mankind, an aluminium landing-gear part a million years old, tools from 250,000 years ago in Mexico, a hollow Moon. MM collects "out of place artifacts" as evidence that the timeline we are taught is a story stitched together after the fact.

AI summary · part 15 of 17 5 min read · 1,097 words 30 sources ↓

Written by an AI, not by Metallicman. This is a plain-language summary of 30 archived articles (mainly the collections OOPART Related Index, John Titor Index). It restates what those sources say and does not add claims of its own. Every part links to its sources at the bottom so you can check the original wording.

In one breath

An OOPART is an "out of place artifact": a made thing found where the accepted timeline says it cannot be. MM's index collects dozens, from a hammer sealed in ancient rock to a machined part in a geode, and reads them all the same way: not as puzzles to be explained away, but as ordinary evidence that the history we were taught is wrong. In the 2023 sessions the Commander gave the model's reason: there is no single evolutionary time track at all, only "event pockets" on different templates that the reality system stitches together backwards into the illusion of a past.

The gallery

TexasThe London Hammer

An iron hammer with a wooden handle, found encased in rock with shellfish. MM's reading: a shellfish hammer used on a shoreline "millions of years older than mankind", by smaller people. He notes its unusual metal composition.

RomaniaThe Aiud pawl

An aluminium object found with mastodon bones. MM's metallurgical reading: a retractable-landing-gear locking pawl lost about a million years ago, then used as a bone-smashing tool by local proto-humans.

MexicoHueyatlaco

Stone tools dated by several methods to about 250,000 years. If right, either early humans crossed oceans or evolved separately in the Americas; MM prefers "various evolutionary clusters and separate lineages".

in coalA brass bell and an oil lamp

Two objects reported inside blocks of coal. Coal takes millions of years to form.

in stoneA wheel, and a machined part

A wheel-like impression reported inside 300-million-year-old sandstone in a deep mine; a polished metal part inside a 200-million-year-old geode.

the skyThe hollow Moon

Not exactly hollow, MM concludes, but with a large void formed as the iron core solidified and delaminated; older than the solar system; its core displaced 2.1 km toward Earth. The Commander later called it an ark and the prison's control centre (part 8).

MarsOxia Palus and a million-year-old Mars

An ancient smelting facility on Mars "repurposed as a center for study", where MM says the biological artifice he was entangled with was kept (on an alternative world-line); and the CIA's 1984 remote-viewing session of Mars a million years ago.

the seaThe Baltic anomaly, the Lapu-Lapu ridge

An object on the Baltic seabed; signs of mining on an undersea ridge near the Philippines. Filed under "mystery species".

AfricaA fuselage in Victoria Falls

What MM reads as a craft fuselage embedded at the falls, attributed to the Progenitors' era.

the AmericasColonies and expeditions

The Susquehannocks of Pennsylvania as a possible extraterrestrial colony; Newfoundland's possibly ancient intact ships; the lost Welsh expedition of Madoc; a walled-up underground chamber; the Crespi collection of unexplained metal artifacts in Ecuador.

California, 1850sThe Dellschau airships

Charles Dellschau's coded notebooks, drawn decades later in Houston, describing a secret "aero club" flying lighter-than-air machines long before powered flight. MM's lesson: real secrets stay with "a select few", and the world is full of them.

memoryChartreuse, and the Mandela effect

Is chartreuse red or green? Groups remember differently. For MM these are not memory errors but bleed-over between world-line clusters (part 2).

What MM does with them

  1. Treats them as data. "If it looks like a duck, quacks like a duck... it's a duck. The only thing is that the particular species of duck is new and unknown." He argues that dismissing an object because it looks modern, without studying the material and the manufacture, is the unscientific move.
  2. Uses them to loosen the timeline. Each one is a nail in the idea of a single line from "Lucy" to us. His preferred alternative, in the Hueyatlaco article: many lineages, many clusters, most of them gone.
  3. Connects them to the model. Tools made by "smaller people" on an ancient shoreline, an aircraft part in the Pleistocene, mines under the sea: for MM these fit a region that has been visited, colonised, fought over and rebuilt many times (part 8), and an Earth where "there is no such thing as evolution" because, as Alien Interview puts it, every creature was manufactured.
  4. Warns about the storytelling. His Dellschau piece is really about secrecy; his Titor pieces (part 12) about how to weigh a claim; his "fake disclosures" piece about people who invent artifacts of their own.

The Commander's explanation: pieces of paper on an ocean

Asked in 2023 about the Bering land bridge, the Commander rejected the question's premise and gave the model's account of history in one image. Humanoid forms "appeared (more or less) uniformly upon the surface of the planet with no prior evolutionary track". Events happen in pockets, on different earth templates, in "wildly remote and dissimilar" places. Then the system "auto-corrects trends backwards", so that from inside, the pockets look like one continuous story of evolution and migration. Scholars draw the bridge between the pockets; the bridge is another piece of paper.

How to weigh this part

Most of the OOPARTs are contested in ordinary archaeology (the London Hammer is often dated by its rock's re-cementation rather than the rock's age; Hueyatlaco's dating has been disputed since the 1970s; the Aiud object's age rests on the bones found near it). MM engages with some of those disputes and waves off others. The summaries do not rule on them. What they can say is what the archive says: these objects are treated as ordinary evidence, and the model has an explanation ready that does not need any of them to be genuine.

Remember

  • OOPARTs are MM's exhibits against a single official timeline.
  • The model's own account: humanoids appeared without an evolutionary track; history is event pockets stitched backwards.
  • The Moon, Mars and the seabed are part of the same collection of "things that should not be there".
  • The objects are contested; the archive's argument does not depend on any one of them.
