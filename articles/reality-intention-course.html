The intention course, class by class

The deep dive on part 5. Everything MM teaches across some fifty articles on affirmation campaigns, arranged as a course: the highway picture, the campaign document, dream boards, on and off cycles, what to expect, what to do when nothing moves, the toolbox of tricks, self-hypnosis, the wish machines and the Silent Sound device, and the full campaign template.

AI summary · part 19 of 40 17 min read · 3,846 words 45 sources ↓

Written by an AI, not by Metallicman. This is a plain-language summary of 45 archived articles (mainly the collections Intention and Prayer Campaigns). It restates what those sources say and does not add claims of its own. Every part links to its sources at the bottom so you can check the original wording.

In one breath

Part 5 gave you the method in a page. This is the whole course. MM wrote about fifty articles on affirmation campaigns between 2019 and 2022, and read together they form twelve lessons: why the first weeks feel like driving off a highway into a swamp; how to build the campaign document and the picture board that goes with it; how to pace the on and off cycles; what "working" looks like (uncomfortable); what to check when nothing moves; a toolbox of named tricks; and the add-ons he experimented with, from a self-hypnosis recording to a soldered "Silent Sound" box that whispers your affirmations under a tone you cannot hear. None of it is magic in his telling. It is steering.

Class 1. The highway picture

MM's favourite way to explain why a campaign hurts at first is a road trip. You were born on a highway to "City A": the pre-birth template from part 4, a comfortable, fated route. A campaign is you deciding to go to City B instead. There is no exit ramp. You leave the tarmac and cross whatever lies between the two roads.

Three numbers frame the whole course. MM counts about four world-lines a second of ordinary living. The distance to a goal is the number of lines between you and it: "easy goals, maybe only a few thousand world-lines; difficult goals, maybe a few billion; impossible goals, maybe a trillion, trillion, trillion." And the time it took him: the quickest campaign result "a matter of weeks", the longest "a little over three years".

Class 2. The rules you keep breaking

Part 5 lists the six fundamentals (thoughts attract, focus amplifies, environment matters). MM also wrote a second list, the six mistakes he sees readers make. Together they are the syllabus.

  1. Do not stop or swap your affirmations. Tweak them, reorder them, make them more colourful, but keep them until the goal is real and "permanent enough". He still reads "I live in a beautiful area that is calm, friendly, has fantastic colors" years after moving to Zhuhai, so that later campaigns cannot displace it.
  2. Do not assume a goal has landed. A man with a cabin and a truck is not yet "a long passionate relationship with a rugged mountain man". "Do not take the cake out of the oven until it is fully baked."
  3. Aim at the end result, not the object. Not a Dior gown but "a gown that fits you perfectly, is comfortable, and that looks stunning on you". Not a thousand dollars but a wallet that always has enough.
  4. Nothing works without the pause. "You do not wind up a toy continuously without letting the spring unwind." Read for a set time, then stop for about the same time.
  5. Everything is interconnected. Cut down the shade tree and your summer electricity bill doubles. Every wish arrives with side effects, so keep the whole list "good, happy and benevolent" and never contradictory.
  6. There is no time. What you prayed for at seven still counts. So he adds one line to every campaign: "These intention prayers supersede any and all previous ones that would conflict with the ones listed here."

Class 3. Building the campaign document

MM's campaign is a password-locked spreadsheet read aloud in a quiet room. In the "structuring" article he publishes the skeleton of his own, with the private lines removed. The order matters: the sections near the top tell your consciousness how to read the rest.

Two writing rules come up in every article. Present tense, always. Not "one day I hope to have", not "I would like", but "I have". And specific about the thing, general about the outcome: "the more unique and special the affirmation is, the quicker it apparently seems to manifest", while vague long-term wishes "just seem to hang there". When you cannot think of anything new to write, keep reading the old list; his stock line for beginners is "I, my family, and my friends are safe, healthy, and live a stable and secure life."

Class 4. The dream board

Words are half the campaign. The other half is a board of pictures: a cork board, or a desktop wallpaper he calls an "intention canvas" or "splash screen". His free sample boards are sorted by what people actually ask for.

the samplesPower, business, suave, health, fame, sex, happiness

Plus one titled "Wine and Cats". "There are no limits", and you can use many clusters of pictures at once.

the legendWrite down what the pictures mean

In his template: the dominant male figure in any image "represents myself"; images of gold and currency mean "large sums of money, wealth and success that I possess"; women in images represent "the situation that I am participating in".

image vocalisationConnect words to pictures on purpose

A board alone is not enough: the spoken affirmations must refer to the images, so the two are "connected together".

warningMedia wishes are not your wishes

"It might have you investing in a $75,000 car instead of spending the year cavorting with pretty girls on the beach." Find out what you want "deep down inside".

Class 5. Pacing the on and off cycles

A campaign has three parts: a reading period, a complete stop of about the same length, then the next campaign with revised lines. MM names the paces after gaits, and gives a second set of names in his "optimisation" article.

Two more pacing ideas. The wet towel: once a week, pick one of your top three affirmations and build a real-world project or event around it, letting it "soak into your reality" like a wet towel on a pad of paper. And positioning: plan a series of campaigns years ahead, each landing a sub-goal, when the destination is far away.

Class 6. What "working" looks like

MM's most repeated warning: when a campaign starts working, life gets worse before it gets better. "Things WILL break. Things WILL go wrong. There WILL be arguments, and it WILL be unfair and very uncomfortable." He treats the strife as a gauge: "the greater the strife, the bigger the changes that are moving towards you." Then come three kinds of signal you have to learn to read.

synchronicityEchoes from nearby lines

His definition: "echoes of similar world-lines that lie off of your present world-line topography." Meaningful coincidences that only you notice. They are progress markers, and they do not come automatically: "you cannot plan for it unless you specifically add it to your affirmation."

false positiveThe 23.6 billion dollar client

He had asked to manage a multi-billion-dollar account. A Chinese-American billionaire turned up wanting to convert stacks of US dollars into RMB, cash for cash, in slices of ten million. It matched the words and missed the intent, so he turned it down. "You are always half way there."

signpostPremonitions you asked for

Birth and death are fixed on the template; between them are "signposts", fixed events you can glimpse in advance. He adds a line asking for them: "I have the ability to have premonitions regarding key events in my life... triggered when I need them." You cannot dodge a signpost; it is "a region of world-lines, like a fence".

the slideWhen the whole scene changes

A slide is an intentional jump off the template. He points to the film The Craft for the feel of it, and to a reader's letter that arrived by email. Slides come with a cost: once off the birth template "you will never be able to return", so campaigns become a lifelong habit.

Class 7. When nothing moves

The "shooting blanks" article is his checklist for a stalled campaign. The first item covers almost everyone.

Time
"For about 95% of the people the reasons why something is not manifesting is that you need to give things time." Your template may simply hold the goal further down the road.
Conflicts
"Be the CEO of a large and important company" and "have a nice calm, happy and peaceful life" were the top two lines on one reader's list. Pick one, get it, then the other. Reality has "inertia".
No slide authorised
The goal is off your template and you never added the lines that allow a slide.
Curses and handicaps
"Many, many, MANY people" put curses on others, often without meaning to; and some blocks are lessons your soul signed up for. The crowbar affirmations below are for this.
A negative partner
"The biggest influence on your life is your partner. They can enable you towards greatness or break you into nothing."
Radiation and fate
The West is "bombarded by brain altering radiation"; and an inauspicious entry point (his BaZi "fate forecasting" articles, part 13) can make some years slow no matter what.
Course corrections
Three options: do nothing and let it run; selectively erase past lines with a superseding affirmation; or "refresh the template" with a fresh campaign that restates everything you want to keep.
De-clutter
Thoughts stick to objects. Step one, throw out anything "hexed, cursed or problematic"; two, purge clothing (an "inertial mass"); three, purge things (about a fifth of them, he guesses, carry a drag); four, bless the property (he wears three blessed items); then tattoos ("an anchor", remove if you can) and time in nature, which "is neutral".

Class 8. The toolbox

By 2021 MM had given names to his tricks. Most are single lines you add to the document.

hammerDivine Hammer

How you read: bold lines louder and with feeling, normal lines normally.

crowbarClear the block

Lines that remove obstacles "placed there by others (maybe even you)", even "your arch enemy in High School... 35 years ago".

tricorderGather intel

Lines asking to be shown what is going on in the non-physical side, so you can "compensate for problems".

grinderSmooth the rough edges

His Cadillac DeVille arrived, then the transmission and air conditioning went: two months' salary. Ask for the income to support the wish, too.

detanglerUntangle old goals

Similar goals from different decades pull on each other; state which one wins.

slipstreamRide with the group

"My affirmations tie together with affirmations of other MM followers so that they all combine with a positive slipstream effect", with a line ignoring any that run counter.

hasp and lockProtect what you have

Lock the parts of life you love so later campaigns cannot move them.

zipperMerge wishes

Three fulfilled wishes (a big white house with windows, meals served, many visitors) can zip together into a hospital ward. Say how they combine.

spellcheckerProofread

Excel did not spell-check his "nice new car" line, and the car that came was not new.

Smaller habits from the "tricks" article: read more than once a day if you like; keep a blessed talisman (he takes the idea from Lynne McTaggart's The Intention Experiment, which he reposts in full); always pray in the same quiet place; never multitask an affirmation; avoid places with "bad vibes", where sad, sick or angry people gather; read your list straight after a Hemi-Sync session (part 13); and touch the people you love. Three add-ons need no document at all: the smile exercise (say something nice to someone every day, with a big smile, before you sleep: "you have traversed 320 world-lines focused on good happy things"), the power boost ("make the people around you happy, and your quanta field will strengthen"), and the lazy man's technique (break a routine with odd, random behaviour to jolt yourself off the groove, with "zero control" over where you land). He also passes on Grabovoi "cheat codes", number strings written or spoken as extra lines, with the shrug: "Whatever you believe will manifest."

Class 9. Self-hypnosis

MM's strongest add-on is a recording. You cannot hypnotise yourself from memory, so you record a script in your own voice, then lie down and listen. He did it daily while running campaigns and says once a week is the minimum. It adds "from 20 minutes to an hour".

Class 10. Machines: wish machines and the Silent Sound box

Because the mechanism is "thought quanta", MM reasons that a machine could keep pushing them for you. He describes two home-made "wish machines": an electronic one (two copper plates sandwiching a photo of yourself with a transparent overlay of the wish; collect, pulse, amplify, filter, broadcast; "make sure that that picture of you does not have any faults, as you will broadcast and amplify those") and a hydraulic one (the same image sandwich, no plates, no electricity, left under running water). Both must stay physically near you, and away from other people's eyes, or their thoughts get mixed in. Then, in late 2021, he went further.

Class 11. Stretch goals, ESP, and the long game

  • The extreme challenge. Keep the list "80% realistic achievable goals, and 20% goals that are further out". Then add one line so extreme that when it lands "you will absolutely know beyond a shadow of a doubt" the campaign did it. Guard it with harmony lines: "All of my intention objectives manifest in harmony with each other" and "At no time are there any discomfort, emotional or physical distress in the process."
  • ESP templates. A base statement that you are developing the skill, a second naming how you learn (visual, auditory, verbal, physical, logical, social or solitary), then lines for one skill at a time from a long menu: astral projection, automatic writing, the "clairs", divination, mediumship, precognition (one MM reader "is a Precog"), psychometry, remote viewing, retrocognition, scrying, telekinesis, telepathy.
  • Remote viewing in this model. Reader BlueNarwhal, who had been corresponding with the Farsight Institute, wrote that remote viewers pick up "cross-talk" from neighbouring world-lines, which is why sessions blur, and that targeting protocols might one day bind a viewer to the most probable lines. MM agreed with the appraisal. His own remote-viewing articles carry the same caveat: what you see may be true on a line next door.
  • The grand strategy. Write the campaign an old person would be glad of, expect to change ("the man that I was when I was in university is not the man who I am today"), add signposts you will recognise (number strings, licence plates, a food, a deja vu), and keep a journal: "it is always enlightening to read your entries from six months ago."
  • Ghost campaigns. "Previous affirmation prayer campaigns never leave, they stay active until they are dismissed." The chewing-gum image: buy it (write the line), chew it (the wait), it loses flavour (mention it again), stick it on the wall (goal reached), it falls off (you stop), and the residue stays. His tyre image: an old campaign is like a set of mud tyres that quietly made last month easier.

Class 12. Reading your own instruments

After prison, MM says he began treating a sudden, strong, clear feeling as information rather than mood: his "situation board". He grades each feeling by strength, hunts for a physical cause first (something he ate, something he did) and only then a non-physical one, and admits he is "eerily accurate" some days and "way off track" on others. The monastery article, from late 2020, opens with the pandemic summer and is where he explains it, alongside a borrowed list of gut instincts never to ignore ("I'm in danger", "this isn't the right choice", "this is it!"). In the course this is the last lesson: the campaign sets the destination, the feelings tell you whether the road is still under the wheels.

What MM says it cannot do, and what he warns against

No campaign can make you another person, break physics, resurrect the dead, or force another consciousness. Keep the document private ("if the thoughts that you generate can build up a life, so can opposition thoughts tear apart a life"). Do not use it to "de-cloak" imprisoned Domain members (part 5). Do not wear or tattoo symbols you have not researched: he lists Goetia sigils and Golden Dawn ritual as real mechanisms that "come with attraction or repellent qualities". And the Silent Sound articles start with a long political preamble about mind-control weapons; the device instructions are his, the preamble is largely quoted from other writers.

Remember

  • You are leaving a highway for a swamp. The swamp is step 2 of 5, and it is where people quit.
  • Document plus board, present tense, specific thing, general outcome, superseding line at the top.
  • Three months on, three off, for beginners. Never stop entirely; keep maintenance lines.
  • Read the signals: strife means movement, look-alikes are not arrivals, coincidences are echoes.
  • Stalled? Ninety-five per cent of the time it is just time.
